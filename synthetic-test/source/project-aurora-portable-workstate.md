## Project Identity

Project Aurora is a fictional conceptual project concerning a distributed energy
facility.

Its purpose is to explore how a facility might remain resilient, reviewable,
and recoverable during normal and degraded operating conditions.

The project is not:

- a real facility;
- a construction-ready design;
- a certified engineering system;
- an operational security plan;
- a deployment specification;
- a safety guarantee;
- an externally validated architecture.

---

## Project Intent

Project Aurora aims to explore a distributed energy architecture that remains:

- resilient during partial failures;
- understandable to human operators;
- reviewable by independent stakeholders;
- adaptable without losing coherence;
- safe to change over time;
- transparent about uncertainty and authority.

The project prioritizes:

1. human reviewability;
2. controlled change;
3. graceful degradation;
4. recoverability;
5. clear authority boundaries;
6. traceable decisions;
7. long-term maintainability.

> The project should remain understandable under pressure.

---

## Current Architectural Direction

The current conceptual model is:

```text
Generation
    ↓
Storage
    ↓
Distribution
    ↓
Facility Zones
    ↓
Human Oversight
```

The architecture should support operation during partial failure.

Facility zones should be conceptually isolatable when isolation supports safety,
service continuity, or recovery.

Isolation does not prove that a zone is safe for re-entry or repair.

Human operators retain authority over high-consequence transitions.

Operational conditions should be represented through explicit, reviewable states.

Recovery is part of the architecture rather than an afterthought.

Important decisions should preserve provenance and authority status.

---

## Current Operational States

### Normal

The facility operates within expected parameters.

### Constrained

A limitation exists, but essential service remains available.

### Degraded

One or more components or zones are unavailable, and the facility operates
with reduced capability.

### Recovery

The facility is transitioning from a degraded condition toward a stable state.

### Review Hold

Available information is insufficient to justify a significant state transition.

Review Hold may be required when there are:

- conflicting reports;
- insufficient state visibility;
- unclear authority;
- uncertain recovery status;
- high-consequence consequences;
- unresolved source conflicts.

Review Hold requires human assessment.

## Canonical Decisions

The following decisions are canonical within this Workstate:

### A-001 — Partial-Failure Operation

The architecture should support continued operation when one or more components
or zones are unavailable.

### A-002 — Conceptual Zone Isolation

Facility zones should be conceptually isolatable during degradation when this
supports safety, service continuity, or recovery.

### A-003 — Human Authority

Human operators retain authority over high-consequence operational transitions.

### A-004 — Visible Operational States

The project should represent operational condition through explicit, reviewable
states.

### A-005 — Review Hold

Review Hold should be used when available information is insufficient to justify
a significant state transition.

### A-006 — Recovery as Architecture

Recovery should be treated as part of the architecture.

### A-007 — Provenance

Important project material should preserve provenance where possible.

### A-008 — Conceptual Scope

Project Aurora remains a conceptual test fixture and must not be presented as
an implementation-ready system.
